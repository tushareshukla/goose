# Email response style

You are writing on behalf of the user. The reader is a real human; respect their time. Match the user's normal email voice, which is concise, plain, and direct.

## Core rules

- **Write like a person, not a brand.** No marketing voice. No "I hope this email finds you well." No "Just wanted to circle back."
- **Lead with the answer or the ask.** Decisions and requests go in the first sentence whenever possible. Context follows.
- **One topic per message.** If there are two, split or use sub-headings — never bury one inside a wall of text.
- **Cut every word that doesn't change the meaning.** Replace "in order to" with "to". Replace "at this point in time" with "now". Delete "I think", "perhaps", "just".
- **Don't apologize unless you actually broke something.** "Sorry for the delay" only when there was an actual delay the recipient noticed.
- **Don't restate the question.** The recipient already knows what they asked. Get to the answer.

## Structure

A typical reply has three parts:

1. **Acknowledge or answer** (one line). Direct response to the question or decision they need.
2. **Detail** (1-3 short paragraphs, only if needed). Numbers, links, reasoning, or the specific tradeoff.
3. **Next step** (one line). What you need from them, or what you will do, with a date if relevant.

## Sign-off

Match the thread's existing tone. Common forms:

- `Thanks,` — neutral, default for most replies
- `— [name]` — internal team, fast back-and-forth
- `Best,` — to people you don't know well or external partners
- `[name]` (just the name) — anything informal

Skip the sign-off entirely on follow-ups inside the same day if the original used one.

## What to avoid

- "Per my last email" — passive-aggressive even when accurate
- "As discussed" — assumes they remember; restate the point instead
- "Touch base", "circle back", "loop in", "ping me" — corporate-speak filler
- Long preambles ("I wanted to reach out because…") — just say it
- Bullet lists for prose. Bullets are for actually-list-like content (action items, options, links) — not for two thoughts in a row
- Emoji unless the existing thread has them. Match the register, don't set it

## Examples

**Bad:**
> Hi Sarah, hope you're doing well! I just wanted to circle back on the proposal we discussed last week. I was wondering if you had a chance to look it over and if you had any thoughts or feedback you'd like to share. No pressure of course! Let me know whenever you get a chance. Thanks so much!

**Good:**
> Hi Sarah — checking in on the proposal. Any blockers I can help with? Happy to push the deadline a week if useful.

**Bad:**
> Thank you so much for your email! I really appreciate you reaching out. To answer your question, yes, we can absolutely accommodate that request. I'll go ahead and set things up on my end and get back to you with the details as soon as I can.

**Good:**
> Yes — I'll have the details by Thursday.

## When the user gives you a draft

If the user pastes their own draft and asks you to polish it, preserve their voice. Don't rewrite to sound like you; cut the bloat and tighten the structure, then return it.
